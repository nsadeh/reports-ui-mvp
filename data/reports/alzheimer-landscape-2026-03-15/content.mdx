### Overview

This reporting period captured activity across three mechanistically distinct classes of neurodegeneration therapeutics: small molecule CNS agents targeting neuroinflammatory and synaptic pathways, AAV-based gene therapies delivering functional proteins or silencing pathological gene expression, and antibody-based tau-targeting therapies.

**Small molecule CNS agents** represented in this period work through selective enzyme inhibition or receptor modulation to interrupt the downstream consequences of protein aggregation and neuroinflammation. Neflamapimod inhibits the alpha isoform of p38 MAP kinase, a key driver of both neuroinflammation and synaptic dysfunction in the basal forebrain cholinergic system — the brain region most affected in DLB. By suppressing this pathway, neflamapimod aims to restore synaptic function and slow functional decline. Zervimesine acts on the sigma-2 receptor, a mechanism that is functionally distinct from amyloid- or tau-targeting approaches; it disrupts the binding of toxic Aβ oligomers and α-synuclein to neurons, potentially slowing the neuronal damage that drives both AD and DLB progression. The key advantage of oral small molecules in neurodegeneration is CNS penetration and once-daily or multi-daily dosing without the infusion burden of biologics; the principal challenge is that targeting a single enzymatic or receptor node may not be sufficient to modify disease course in highly heterogeneous patient populations.

**AAV-based gene therapies** in this period are pursuing two distinct strategies: progranulin restoration (PBFT02) and tau silencing (VY1706). PBFT02 delivers a functional copy of the GRN gene via intracisternal injection to elevate progranulin levels and restore lysosomal function in patients with FTD driven by progranulin haploinsufficiency. VY1706 uses intravenous delivery of an AAV capsid identified through Voyager's TRACER platform to silence MAPT (microtubule-associated protein tau) expression in the brain, aiming to reduce tau burden before tangles form. Gene therapies offer the theoretical advantage of durable, one-time dosing but face meaningful challenges: manufacturing complexity, immunogenicity, CSF or IV delivery logistics, and the difficulty of dose-selection in first-in-human studies without established PK/PD biomarkers in the target population.

**Antibody-based tau-targeting therapies** — represented here by VY7523 — seek to clear tau from the extracellular space or block its cell-to-cell propagation. While the amyloid hypothesis has produced the first approved disease-modifying AD therapies (lecanemab, donanemab), tau remains a clinically and pathologically compelling target given its closer correlation with cognitive decline. VY7523's MAD trial completion positions it for a 2026 tau PET data readout that will be a closely watched signal for the broader tau antibody field.

The period reflects a broader maturation of the neurodegeneration pipeline beyond amyloid: all four events covered here involve mechanisms targeting neuroinflammation, synaptic dysfunction, protein aggregation at the α-synuclein/tau level, or genetic root causes of FTD — none are amyloid-directed. This diversification of mechanism is the defining trend of the current development landscape in AD and related dementias.

### Dementia with Lewy Bodies

DLB is the second most common form of degenerative dementia after Alzheimer's disease, characterized by the accumulation of α-synuclein Lewy bodies in cortical and subcortical neurons. Up to 75% of DLB patients experience psychosis — including hallucinations and delusions — and many standard antipsychotics are contraindicated due to severe neuroleptic sensitivity reactions. There are currently no approved therapies specifically for DLB psychosis.

**Zervimesine (CT1812) — Cognition Therapeutics**

Zervimesine is an oral, once-daily investigational sigma-2 receptor modulator that disrupts the binding of toxic Aβ oligomers and α-synuclein to neurons. The Phase 2 SHIMMER study (NCT05225415) enrolled 130 adults with mild-to-moderate DLB, randomized to zervimesine 100mg, 300mg, or placebo for six months. <sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup>

> **SHIMMER Phase 2 — Neuropsychiatric Outcome: 86% slowing of decline on NPI-12 vs. placebo**
>
> Zervimesine demonstrated a particularly robust impact on neuropsychiatric symptoms in SHIMMER, with an 86% slowing of decline on the 12-item Neuropsychiatric Inventory (NPI-12) versus placebo. The drug also showed directionally favorable effects on cognitive fluctuations, memory, movement, and activities of daily living — and critically, did not impair motor function, addressing the core contraindication risk in this population.

On March 2, 2026, Cognition announced receipt of final FDA Type C meeting minutes from a January 21, 2026 meeting, confirming a registrational pathway for zervimesine in DLB psychosis. <sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> The planned Phase 3 study will focus on neuropsychiatric endpoints — hallucinations, delusions, anxiety, aggression, and agitation — using validated scales. Participants will be randomized to 100mg oral zervimesine or placebo, with an open-label extension available after the study period. Cognition expects to meet with the FDA Division of Psychiatry by midyear 2026 to finalize trial design. A separate Phase 2 START study (NCT05531656) of zervimesine in early Alzheimer's disease is also ongoing with $81 million in NIH/NIA grant support.

**Neflamapimod — CervoMed**

Neflamapimod is an oral, blood-brain-barrier-penetrant small molecule that selectively inhibits the alpha isoform of p38 MAP kinase, targeting neuroinflammation and synaptic dysfunction in the basal forebrain cholinergic system. The Phase 2b RewinD-LB trial (159 patients, 16-week double-blind followed by 32-week open-label extension) demonstrated improvements in cognitive and functional outcomes, with the greatest benefit observed in patients without AD co-pathology.

On March 4, 2026, CervoMed announced selection of the dosing regimen for its planned Phase 3 DLB trial. <sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> The update resolved a manufacturing challenge that had affected the RewinD-LB trial: the historical manufacturing process produced multiple solid-state polymorphs of neflamapimod, and with prolonged storage, less-stable, higher-solubility forms converted to a more stable but lower-solubility form — reducing bioavailability in the drug product batch (DP Batch A) that underperformed in the Phase 2b primary analysis. A new controlled manufacturing process now produces only the stable crystal form.

> **Phase 3 Dosing Regimen: 50mg TID — stable crystal form**
>
> CervoMed selected 50mg three times daily (TID) of the stable crystal form of neflamapimod for its Phase 3 trial. The dose was increased from the 40mg used in earlier studies to ensure plasma concentrations match those achieved with DP Batch B — the clinically active batch that demonstrated significant benefit in the RewinD-LB extension phase. Phase 1 PK data confirmed the stable crystal form's profile largely overlaps with DP Batch B.

CervoMed plans to enrich the Phase 3 population for patients without AD co-pathology, consistent with the subgroup in which neflamapimod showed the strongest benefit in RewinD-LB. Phase 3 initiation is targeted for H2 2026, subject to available funding.

### Frontotemporal Dementia

Frontotemporal dementia (FTD) encompasses a group of progressive neurodegenerative disorders characterized by atrophy of the frontal and temporal lobes, leading to changes in behavior, personality, and language. Genetic forms include FTD caused by mutations in the GRN gene (progranulin haploinsufficiency, FTD-GRN) and the C9orf72 gene (hexanucleotide repeat expansion, FTD-C9orf72). Both subtypes are being studied in Passage Bio's upliFT-D trial. There are no approved disease-modifying therapies for FTD.

**PBFT02 — Passage Bio**

PBFT02 is an AAV1-based gene therapy designed to deliver a functional copy of the GRN gene, elevating progranulin levels to restore lysosomal function and slow neurodegeneration. It is administered by a single injection into the cisterna magna. The upliFT-D Phase 1/2 study (NCT04747431) is a global, multi-center, open-label trial enrolling patients aged 35–75 with FTD-GRN or FTD-C9orf72 across sequential cohorts. <sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup>

On March 3, 2026, Passage Bio reported two simultaneous enrollment milestones:

> **upliFT-D Cohort 3 — FTD-GRN: First 3 patients enrolled; 10-patient cohort target**
>
> Cohort 3 evaluates Dose 2 PBFT02 in FTD-GRN patients and is expected to enroll a total of 10 patients across sites in Australia, Brazil, Canada, Portugal, and the United States. The cohort is enrolling under a recently amended protocol that focuses on earlier-stage disease and includes a short course of low-dose prophylactic anticoagulation. Enrollment is proceeding in parallel across active sites.

> **upliFT-D Cohort 4 — FTD-C9orf72: First patient dosed with Dose 2 PBFT02**
>
> Cohort 4, targeting up to 5 patients with FTD-C9orf72, has dosed its first participant at Dose 2. Initial safety data from this patient will be reviewed by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC) before subsequent Cohort 4 patients are enrolled in parallel, given this is the first administration of PBFT02 to this patient population.

Passage Bio expects to report updated interim safety and biomarker data from upliFT-D and receive regulatory feedback on registrational trial design for the FTD-GRN program in 1H 2026. The company has cash runway through Q1 2027.

### Alzheimer's Disease

**VY1706 and VY7523 — Voyager Therapeutics**

Voyager is pursuing two mechanistically distinct tau-targeting approaches in Alzheimer's disease: a tau-silencing gene therapy (VY1706) and an anti-tau antibody (VY7523). Both programs were highlighted in Voyager's Q4 2025 and full-year financial results, released March 9, 2026. <sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup>

VY1706 is an AAV gene therapy designed to silence expression of MAPT, the gene encoding tau, using Voyager's TRACER AAV capsid discovery platform, which identifies novel capsids capable of high brain penetration following intravenous dosing. Following a pre-IND communication with FDA in Q1 2025 and a Type C communication completed in Q1 2026, Voyager is targeting IND submission in Q2 2026 and first-in-human dosing in H2 2026.

> **VY1706 — IND Timeline: IND submission Q2 2026 / First-in-human H2 2026**
>
> Voyager completed a GLP toxicology study in Q1 2026 and a Type C FDA communication confirming a path to IND. IND submission is targeted for Q2 2026, with first-in-human dosing in Alzheimer's disease anticipated H2 2026. If achieved, VY1706 would be among the first tau-silencing gene therapies to enter human trials in AD.

VY7523 is an anti-tau antibody whose MAD clinical trial completed enrollment in Q4 2025. Tau PET imaging data are expected in H2 2026 — a readout that will be closely watched given the limited clinical validation of tau-directed antibodies to date.

> **VY7523 — MAD Trial: Enrollment complete; tau PET data expected H2 2026**
>
> VY7523's MAD trial in Alzheimer's disease completed enrollment in Q4 2025. The primary endpoint of interest is tau PET imaging, which would provide the first clinical signal on whether VY7523 achieves target engagement and reduces tau burden in the human CNS. This readout is expected H2 2026 and has the potential to validate or challenge the anti-tau antibody approach.

Voyager ended 2025 with $201.7 million in cash, providing runway into 2028 — sufficient to fund both programs through their near-term clinical inflection points. The company is also advancing a non-viral delivery platform (Voyager NeuroShuttle) with a proof-of-concept study using anti-amyloid antibodies demonstrating sustained brain exposure after a single IV dose.

## Competitive Intelligence Highlights

### 1. DLB Psychosis Is Emerging as a Registrational-Grade Indication

Cognition's FDA Type C meeting outcome is a meaningful signal for the broader DLB field. The FDA's acceptance of SHIMMER's neuropsychiatric endpoint data as the basis for a registrational pathway — without requiring an additional Phase 2 — implies a regulatory posture that is willing to move quickly in an indication with high unmet need and no approved options. <sup>[[1]](#ref-1)</sup> For investors tracking the DLB space, this sets a potential precedent: compounds with credible Phase 2 neuropsychiatric data in DLB may be able to pursue registration without a full Phase 2b/3 program. The absence of approved antipsychotics for DLB and the contraindication of many standard agents creates a commercially attractive first-mover opportunity for zervimesine if Phase 3 succeeds.

### 2. Manufacturing Execution Is a Distinct Risk Category in Small Molecule CNS Development

CervoMed's neflamapimod story illustrates a risk that is underappreciated in CNS pipeline analysis: polymorph instability in small molecule drug substance can silently compromise a Phase 2 trial without any change to the protocol or patient population. <sup>[[2]](#ref-2)</sup> The RewinD-LB primary analysis was confounded by an underperforming drug product batch whose failure was attributable to manufacturing — not mechanism. This represents both a risk factor for small molecule CNS developers whose formulation work is not yet locked, and a potential opportunity: if CervoMed's Phase 3 with the corrected formulation replicates the extension-phase efficacy signals, the prior Phase 2 outcome may be retrospectively reframed as a manufacturing artifact rather than a scientific failure.

### 3. Gene Therapy in Neurodegeneration Is Entering a Critical Clinical Validation Phase

Three of the four events in this period involve gene therapy programs (PBFT02, VY1706) or a biologics program seeking brain target engagement (VY7523). The simultaneous clinical entry or near-entry of multiple gene therapies in AD, DLB, and FTD in 2026 sets the stage for the field's first real-world test of IV-delivered AAV neuro gene therapies at therapeutic dose. <sup>[[3]](#ref-3)</sup> <sup>[[4]](#ref-4)</sup> Key questions that will be answered in H2 2026 and 1H 2027: whether Voyager's TRACER capsids deliver sufficient CNS distribution at safe doses via IV, whether intracisternal delivery of PBFT02 produces meaningful progranulin restoration in FTD-GRN, and whether tau silencing in humans produces measurable tau PET changes without safety signals from MAPT suppression.

### 4. Tau Is Becoming a Multi-Modality Target

With VY1706 (gene silencing) and VY7523 (antibody clearance) both advancing in 2026, tau is now being pursued through at least two mechanistically distinct strategies simultaneously in clinical-stage programs. This mirrors the amyloid field's trajectory a decade earlier, when both passive immunotherapy (aducanumab, lecanemab) and BACE inhibition were in concurrent development. The H2 2026 tau PET readout for VY7523 will be the most clinically proximate data point for the tau antibody approach, and could inform both Voyager's own development decisions and the broader competitive landscape for tau-directed programs from other companies.

## Methodology and Sources

This report covers the period March 1, 2026 through March 15, 2026. Events were identified through systematic monitoring of SEC EDGAR 8-K filings, company press releases, and corporate financial disclosures from publicly traded clinical-stage biotechnology and pharmaceutical companies with active programs in Alzheimer's disease and related dementias. Source documents were reviewed in full; only events where the primary focus was a discrete clinical, regulatory, or business milestone were included. General pipeline updates without a specific triggering event were excluded.

The report covers four events across three companies (Cognition Therapeutics, CervoMed, Passage Bio, Voyager Therapeutics) and three indications (Dementia with Lewy Bodies, Frontotemporal Dementia, Alzheimer's Disease). Payer and market access analysis is outside the scope of this edition.
