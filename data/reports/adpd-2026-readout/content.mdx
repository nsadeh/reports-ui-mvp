## Executive Summary

This report analyzes 41 clinical abstracts presented at AD/PD 2026, spanning preclinical mechanistic data, Phase 1–3 trial updates, and a substantial body of real-world evidence, with biomarker-focused readouts representing the single largest category of update type. Anti-amyloid immunotherapy continues to dominate the competitive landscape, with lecanemab and donanemab generating the greatest volume of data across real-world cohorts, long-term efficacy extensions, and safety surveillance, underscoring their entrenched positions as the current standard of care in early Alzheimer's disease. Valiltramiprosate (ALZ-801, Alzheon) emerges as the most clinically active late-stage challenger, presenting multiple Phase 3 readouts demonstrating significant cognitive benefits, hippocampal atrophy reversal, and a differentiated safety profile in APOE4/4 homozygotes, a population historically underserved by anti-amyloid antibodies due to elevated ARIA risk. Real-world data from lecanemab and donanemab reveal an increasingly granular picture of treated populations, with donanemab cohorts skewing older and predominantly female, and lecanemab data from Chinese populations confirming tolerability and meaningful amyloid clearance, signaling growing ex-US commercial relevance. A notable and potentially disruptive signal from one real-world lecanemab abstract reported global cognitive decline and memory worsening despite frontal function improvement, which warrants close monitoring as post-approval safety evidence matures. Biomarker innovation represents a cross-cutting theme, with plasma pTau217, GFAP, amyloid PET negativity rates, and novel EEG-based predictors of adverse events all advancing as tools for patient stratification and treatment monitoring. Several earlier-stage programs merit attention, including the JAK inhibitor baricitinib for neuroinflammation, the CB2 agonist NTRX-07, and the muscarinic agent xanomeline/trospium targeting AD-related agitation, reflecting a broadening mechanistic aperture beyond amyloid clearance. The pipeline as a whole signals a field in transition, where amyloid immunotherapy dominance is being challenged not only by efficacy and safety differentiation strategies but by growing investment in inflammation, tau, and non-amyloid pathways as combination or alternative targets.

<summary><strong>1. Resting-state EEG as a Tool to Monitor Early Electrophysiological Changes During Lecanemab Infusion</strong></summary>

<div class="abstract-content">
<p class="abstract-title">Resting-state EEG as a Tool to Monitor Early Electrophysiological Changes During Lecanemab Infusion</p>
<p class="abstract-authors"><em>Yumei Liu, Shaoxia Fan, Shuhan Fan, Zian Pei, Zhifan Zhou, Xue Shi, Yi Guo</em></p>
<p class="abstract-affiliations">1Shenzhen Bay laboratory, Institute Of Neurological And Psychiatric Disorders, Shenzhen, China, 2Shenzhen People&#x27;s Hospital, Department Of Neurology, shenzhen, China, 3Southern University of Science and Technology, Department Of Electronic And Electrical Engineering, shenzhen, China</p>

<p><strong>Aims:</strong> Lecanemab, an anti-amyloid-β monoclonal antibody, slows cognitive decline in Alzheimer’s disease (AD). However, its early electrophysiological effects remain unclear. This study examined early resting-state electroencephalograms (rsEEG) changes after Lecanemab infusion and assessed whether EEG could predict adverse effects.</p>
<p><strong>Methods:</strong> We prospectively collected rsEEG from AD patients before their first Lecanemab infusion, and at days 1, 3, 7, 1 month, and 3 months post-infusion. Of 32 eligible patients, usable rsEEG was obtained from 25, 20, 18, 22, and 19, respectively. Power spectral density and source localization changes were analyzed.</p>
<p><strong>Results:</strong> At baseline, higher theta power correlated with lower MMSE (ρ= -0.38) and MoCA (ρ= -0.42), and higher ADL scores (ρ = 0.43). Lower alpha power was linked to higher CDR (ρ= -0.38) and lower MMSE (ρ = 0.38). DTABR(delta + theta/alpha +beta) correlated positively with CDR (ρ= 0.41) and ADL (ρ= 0.40), and negatively with MMSE (ρ = -0.43) and MoCA (ρ = -0.45). Longitudinally, delta power increased, peaking at day 7 (0.23 ± 0.02 vs. 0.28 ± 0.03; Cohen’s d = 0.89), with sources localized to bilateral parietal/temporal and left occipital regions; these changes attenuated by 1 and 3 months. Delta activity and DTABR correlated with Hypertension-Coronary score (ρ = 0.51) and cognitive decline duration (ρ = 0.55). Patients with adverse events (ARIA, infusion reactions, dizziness, allergy) had lower baseline alpha power than those without (0.26 ± 0.02 vs. 0.31 ± 0.03; η²p = 0.137).</p>
<p><strong>Conclusions:</strong> Lecanemab induces early electrophysiological changes in AD, marked by increased delta activity and DTABR within the first week. RsEEG is sensitive in detecting these alterations and may serve as a noninvasive biomarker for both treatment monitoring and predicting adverse events.</p>

<div class="abstract-content">
<p class="abstract-title">AI-Driven Digital Speech Biomarkers Detect Therapeutic Effects of Transcranial Pulse Stimulation in Alzheimer’s Disease</p>
<p class="abstract-authors"><em>Daniel Wiechmann,2, Ali Günes, Elma Kerz, Yu Qiao, Martin Köhne, Ulrich Sprick</em></p>

<p><strong>Aims:</strong> To develop interpretable AI models using digital biomarkers derived from spontaneous speech to differential diagnosis of Alzheimer’s disease (AD) and to individually quantify therapeutic effects of transcranial pulse stimulation (TPS) on disease progression.</p>
<p><strong>Methods:</strong> End-to-end automatic classification models were trained using spontaneous speech data from 40 patients with clinically diagnosed AD (mean age = 73.8 ± 9.9 years) and 27 healthy controls. Diagnosis was based on current German S3 guidelines, including comprehensive neuropsychological assessments (CERAD Plus including MMSE, Stroop-Test) and structural MRI. Interpretable AI identified the most discriminative speech features associated with AD pathology. Digital speech biomarkers were then used to quantify TPS-related modulation of disease trajectory related to 14 TPS- treated patients, based on paired recordings acquired 268.7 ± 152.9 days apart.</p>
<p><strong>Results:</strong> Speech biomarkers showed strong correlations with cognitive performance (CERAD r = 0.72; MMSE r = 0.73) and outperformed the Stroop test in capturing cognitive impairment. Classification accuracy for AD versus healthy controls achieved F1 = 0.81. At the group level, patients receiving TPS showed stable or slightly improved linguistic performance throughout the follow-up period, contrasting with the ~3.3-point annual MMSE decline expected in untreated Alzheimer’s disease. TPS intervention also revealed pronounced individual variability in t0/t1-speech biomarker trajectories, indicating heterogeneous patient responsiveness. Key discriminative speech features identified by the interpretable AI models yield mechanistic insights into TPS-related neurophysiological effects.</p>
<p><strong>Conclusions:</strong> This study introduces a novel framework integrating interpretable AI with speech-derived digital biomarkers as precision tools for AD diagnosis and monitoring. Findings demonstrate that TPS exerts personalized, measurable effects on disease progression, detectable through sensitive speech-based metrics which surpass traditional cognitive assessments.</p>
